# Known: 5-minute speaker script

Team E497 · Premium Travel · Cathay Hackathon 2026

One block per click of the deck (`E497_Known_presentation.html`, 19 steps). Right arrow or the pager at the bottom right moves on. Every part of the story is a PROBLEM slide followed by its SOLUTION, and every solution is the same thing: someone talks to Known, Known picks a skill, a card appears.

**Length:** about 600 words. With a second and a half for each click that is 4 min 55 s at a calm 135 words a minute, so there is a little slack. If you run long, shorten the roadmap step.

**Pace:** on every solution step the phone plays for about 10 seconds (the sentence, the thinking, the steps, the card, the answer), and the result card is up about 5 seconds after you click. Start speaking as you click. The phone finishes before your line does, so you never have to wait for it.

**Who speaks:** a natural split for three people is steps 1 to 8, steps 9 to 16, steps 17 to 19.

## The script

| # | On screen | Running time | Say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | 0:00 to 0:17 | Hello, we’re All Aboard, team E497. Known is a voice assistant in the Cathay app for passengers who need extra help. You explain what you need once, and it’s remembered on every trip after that. |
| 2 | CASE STUDY · Mei Lau, aged 8 | 0:17 to 0:32 | To make it concrete, we followed one passenger. Mei Lau is eight, she’s autistic and she uses a wheelchair, and she’s flying from Hong Kong to Seoul with her parents. |

| 5 | SOLUTION · Booking (Listen and plan) | 1:03 to 1:16 | Here she describes Mei in her own words. Known picks out three needs and records them using the assistance codes Cathay staff already work with. |
| 6 | SOLUTION · Booking (What I remember) | 1:16 to 1:32 | It also remembers them, so she doesn’t have to repeat this next time. She can see, change or delete anything it has kept, and it stores what helps Mei rather than her diagnosis. |

| 10 | SOLUTION · How Known knows (the terminal, with its sensors) | 2:21 to 2:33 | Known estimates how busy each area is, starting with flight times, which Cathay already has. It works with counts only, never faces or recordings. |
| 11 | SOLUTION · Calm places (3D map of the terminal) | 2:33 to 2:45 | When mum asks for a quiet toilet near the gate, it shows her a calm one on a 3D map, about a minute away. |
| 12 | SOLUTION · Calm route (3D map of the terminal) | 2:45 to 2:58 | Next she asks for the Sensory Corner. The shortest way runs past the queue at Gate 26, so Known takes them a step-free route around it. |
| 13 | PROBLEM · Gate change, today | 2:58 to 3:11 | Then the gate changes. Usually you learn that from a loud announcement, and sudden noise is one of the hardest things for autistic travellers. |
| 14 | SOLUTION · Gate change (Gate watch) | 3:11 to 3:23 | Known lets the family know first, with a silent alert and a quiet route to the new gate. The staff there already have Mei’s card. |
| 15 | PROBLEM · At home, today | 3:23 to 3:35 | Meanwhile Mei’s grandmother is following from home. The app only gives her the flight status, so she can’t tell whether Mei has been met. |
| 16 | SOLUTION · At home (Keep family posted) | 3:35 to 3:47 | With Known she can ask whether they’ve boarded. It shows her how far they’ve got and tells her once they’re on the plane. |

The first is higher customer satisfaction: passengers who need extra help get personal, consistent support with less effort. The second is lower service costs, because there are fewer repeated enquiries and less coordination rework. The third is stronger loyalty, since a family that can depend on this support has a reason to choose Cathay again. | 19 | Close | 4:46 to 4:55 | That’s Known. We’d welcome two weeks of discovery with your team as a first step. Thank you. |

## Delivery notes

- **Every solution step plays the same five beats on the phone:** the words appear as they are spoken, Known thinks, it names the skill and shows "Working on it" with its steps, the card builds itself, and Known answers in one line. Name the skill out loud once ("this is its Arrange help skill") so the judges learn to read the chip.
- **Step 4 (Meet Known):** Known waves first. Wait for the wave, then speak. Do not read the seven lines on the card.
- **Step 5:** the three phrases Known picked out are underlined in the sentence, then fly down and become the three tasks. Point at one as it lands.
- **Step 5:** the plan ends with a mint strip, "Saved to memory", with a lock. Point at it on step 6, as you say "It also remembers them".
- **Step 6 (What I remember):** this is the privacy slide. Point at the greyed row, "Diagnosis: not kept", and at the three small promises under it.
- **From step 8 on, every skill starts with a mint line, "Remembered".** Point at it once, on step 8: that line is the memory at work.
- **Step 8:** the family walks in and Wing meets them in the 3D scene while the phone books the help. Let both play.
- **Steps 10 to 12 are one move.** On step 10 point at the counters and the flight board. On steps 11 and 12 the phone shows the team’s 3D map of Hong Kong airport: the camera flies in, the amber and red patch is the crowd, a thin dashed line shows the shortest way through it, and then a bold green line draws the calm way from "You" to the pin, bending round the crowd. Trace that bend with your finger: it is the point of the slide. Wait for the map before you speak about it.
- **Step 14 is one card.** The gate board flips from 24 to 41 about two seconds after you click, and the 3D route follows a second later. Calm mode, the pictures for Mei, is no longer shown on this slide. It is still one of the seven skills on the Meet Known card, so if a judge asks, say it is the screen meant for Mei herself: three pictures of what happens next.
- **Step 16 (grandma):** let the journey line draw down to "At Gate 41" before you say "tells her once they’re on the plane".
- **Step 17:** this is the business slide. Pause briefly after each of the three benefits, and point at its card as you name it. Do not talk about proof here: the next slide, the milestones, is where you say how it would be proved.
- **Step 18:** point along the five cards from left to right.
- Point at the phone, not at the 3D scene. Known is what you are selling.

## Likely questions, and short answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is Known working today? | No. The conversations in the deck are scripted: that is milestone 0. A working Known is built and tested in milestone 2, before any real family uses it. |
| Cathay already has a chatbot. How is this different? | A chatbot answers questions. Known does the task: it books the help, plans the route and watches the gate, and it is built for passengers who need assistance. It could live inside Cathay’s own assistant as a set of skills. |
| What if Known mishears? | It shows the plan before anything is sent, and the parent or guardian confirms it. Nothing goes to staff unseen. |
| Is the waiting claim real? | It comes from our conversation with Cathay ground staff in October 2026. We would confirm volumes in the pilot. |
| What about privacy? | The family says what to share and sees the brief card before it is sent. No diagnosis is stored, only what helps. |
| How does the memory work? | An in-house memory layer, run inside Cathay’s own systems. Each fact is kept per passenger and per trip, every read is logged, and trip notes can carry an expiry date. |
| Is the memory secure? | It is designed so the family sees everything Known keeps, staff see only the card the family approves, and no diagnosis is stored. It would start with a privacy impact assessment. |
| Is the 3D map real? | It is our own model of Hong Kong airport with its real gate numbers and a route finder. The route finder gives the straight way. The crowd patches, and the bend the path takes round them, are what Known would add, and are an illustration here. |
| What changes for staff? | One brief card per passenger: what helps and what to avoid. No new system to log into. |
| Who are the ground staff? Is it ALLWAYS? | ALLWAYS is the example on the attendant’s label. Known does not depend on who does the job: it sends one brief card to whoever meets the family. Milestone 1 agrees that with Cathay. |
| How big is the need? | IATA reports about 30% more wheelchair requests a year at bigger airports, and says many come from travellers who mainly need help finding the way. In Hong Kong, 534,200 people had a disability in 2020: 7.1% (Census and Statistics Department). |
| What are you promising? | A decision at every step, not a saving: go or no-go. In the test we compare today’s phone call, a plain form and Known. If the plain form does the job as well as Known, that is a valid result. |
| How is the pilot measured? | On four things: did Known hear the need right the first time, did the family have to say anything twice, did help come when Known said, and staff minutes. Staff check every plan Known makes. |
| What does it cost? | One assistant inside the app and a card for staff. No aircraft changes and no new booking codes. |
| What is the Sensory Corner? | A free quiet room in Terminal 1 at Hong Kong airport, opened in April 2026 with Heep Hong Society. |
| Does the crowd map exist today? | The parts do. Airports count people with sensors, and forecast crowding from flight schedules. Orlando’s app is reported to show crowd levels. We found nobody who routes one passenger by a personal sensory profile. Our first version needs no new sensors: flight times and a parent’s profile. |
| Is that surveillance? | It keeps a number per zone. No faces, no device identities, no recordings. It would start with a privacy impact assessment, as Hong Kong’s privacy regulator asks for camera analytics. |

## Check these before you present

These lines rest on your own notes or on choices made for the story. Confirm or change them.

1. **Known is shown as a scripted conversation.** Nothing on the phone is a live model. Say "this is how it would work", not "this is running now".
1. **The memory and its privacy lines are design commitments, not a built system** (steps 5, 6 and the "Remembered" lines). Say "designed to". Do not say "encrypted" or "compliant" as facts. Say "an in-house memory layer". No product is named, because none has been chosen.
1. **The 3D map on the phone (steps 11, 12 and 14)** is the team’s own wayfinder model. Gates 24, 26, 30, 40 and 41 are real gate numbers, but the Sensory Corner is placed at Gate 30 only for the story, the bend round the crowd is drawn by the deck and not computed by the wayfinder, and the walking times on the cards (8 and 12 minutes) are illustrative, not what the wayfinder computes.
2. **"They can wait up to an hour"** (step 7). From your notes of the staff conversation. There is no public source, so present it as what staff told you.
3. **"Few families know, and it is hard to find"** (step 9). This is your team’s observation. The source line on the slide cites what could be verified: the airport opened the room in April 2026, and the Cathay app’s text has no mention of it.
4. **"The Cathay app shows flight status, with no status for assistance"** (step 15) was checked against the app’s own text.
5. **Cathay’s existing chatbot.** If a judge names it, do not argue about what it can do today. Use the answer above: Known does tasks, and could be a set of skills inside it.
6. **ALLWAYS.** It now appears only on the attendant’s label in step 8 ("Wing, from ALLWAYS"). The shared Cathay and ALLWAYS board is gone from the deck. Your implementation plan says the relationship is not publicly confirmed, so say "in our example".
7. **The airline code on the case slide** now reads DPNA and WCHS (cannot manage steps), to match the plan card on step 5. It read WCHC before. Use whichever your team means, and say so if it should change back.
8. **The route and names are illustrative:** flight CX 410 to Seoul, Wing, the 07:00 meeting time, Gates 24, 26 and 41, the 12-minute walk. The deck says so in its footnote.
9. **The pilot numbers** (one airport, 30 to 50 cases, 12 weeks, the milestone dates) are planning estimates from your implementation plan. No role has been assigned or approved. Say "we plan" and "if approved".
10. **The comfort map (steps 10 to 12) is a design, not a working system.** What is real: airports already count people with sensors and camera analytics (Heathrow reuses its own cameras, LaGuardia Terminal B has more than 45 sensors), and vendors forecast crowding from flight schedules. What is not proven: nobody has published how accurate a forecast is for a walkway, no sensor in the research measures noise across a terminal, and Hong Kong airport has no public crowd feed for airlines. Say "we would start with flight times" and "with the airport’s counters", not "we use the airport’s live data". Do not say "noise heatmap", "detects when Mei is uncomfortable" or "first in the world".
11. **Sensory Corner details.** Sources agree it opened in April 2026 in Terminal 1 and is free, but differ on its exact position and hours. The concourse on the slides (Gates 24 and 26, the café, the toilets) is an illustration, not a plan of the real terminal.
12. **The cover** now shows the team number and name (E497, All Aboard) and the five members’ names, family name first as on the hackathon’s team page: Uh Suh Yeon, Shin Hyunsung, Kang Seungwan, Shin Seung Min, Kim Hyunjun. Check the spelling of each name.
